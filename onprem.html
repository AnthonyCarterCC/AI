On-premise AI

Run AI in your own office.

Free, open-source AI that runs on a computer you own, in your own building. No monthly fee per person, and your documents never leave the room.

What it is

An LLM, or large language model, is the engine behind ChatGPT, Claude and Copilot. Most are rented from the cloud. Some are published for anyone to download and run for free. These are called open-weight or open-source models.

“On-premise” means you run one of them on a computer in your own office. Staff open a chat page in their web browser, much like ChatGPT, but the thinking happens on that one machine.

  • Your data stays putContracts, client files and staff records are never sent to anyone else’s servers. For many businesses this is the real reason to do it.
  • No per-person feeThe software and models are free. You pay for the computer and the power, not for each user.
  • Works without the internetIt keeps working if the connection drops, and it is never subject to a provider’s price rise.

One limitation: the free models you can run on an office PC are good, but not as clever as the biggest paid ones. Think of a very capable graduate, not your best senior specialist.

So what does it actually cost?

The models cost nothing. The software to run them (Ollama and Open WebUI) is free too. Your real costs are three things: the computer, the electricity (roughly A$25 to A$40 a month for a machine that is on all day and busy part of it) and someone’s time to set it up and look after it.

Some organisations have a policy against models made in certain countries. That applies to Qwen and DeepSeek. Because they run in your office, your data does not go to their makers, but check your own policy before you choose.

What you need to run them

One thing matters most: the graphics card.

Forget processor speeds and spec sheets. What decides which AI you can run is the video memory on the graphics card. More memory lets you run a bigger, cleverer model.

About A$5,000

The entry machine

Graphics card with 16 GB of video memory (such as a GeForce RTX 5070 Ti) and 32 GB of ordinary memory.

  • Runs the small and mid-size models comfortably
  • Good for two or three people at once
  • Handles everyday drafting, summarising and questions

Cost: about A$5,000 for the complete machine.

About A$7,500

The middle machine

Graphics card with 24 GB of video memory (such as a GeForce RTX 4090, often bought used) and 64 GB of ordinary memory.

  • Runs the models in the 20 to 35 billion size range
  • Good for four or five people at once
  • Room for longer documents

Cost: about A$7,500 for the complete machine.

About A$10,000

The top machine

Graphics card with 32 GB of video memory (a GeForce RTX 5090) and 128 GB of ordinary memory.

  • Fastest answers and the most room for long documents
  • Good for around five to eight people at once
  • The most capable model a single consumer card can run

Cost: about A$10,000. The RTX 5090 alone was about A$6,000 in Australia in March 2026.

What separates them: the graphics card and the ordinary memory. The rest of each machine (processor, fast solid-state drive, cooling, power-protection unit) is much the same. All prices are rough Australian-dollar estimates; graphics-card and memory prices have been rising, so check current prices.

The end result

What your team actually gets.

A private chat page that lives on your office network. Staff open it in their browser, type a question and get an answer, with nothing sent outside the building.

Works well for

  • Summarising long contracts, tenders, policies and meeting notes
  • Drafting and tidying emails, letters and reports
  • Answering questions about your own documents once they are loaded in
  • Rewriting, translating and turning notes into tables
  • Helping with scripts and spreadsheet formulas

Does less well at

  • Hard analysis and tricky, multi-step problems compared with the best paid tools
  • Building applications from scratch
  • Anything that needs today’s news or the live web
  • Working inside Outlook, Teams and SharePoint the way Copilot does
  • Very long documents with several people using it at once

A typical day: a manager drops a 40-page supplier contract into the chat, asks for the unusual clauses, and has a useful first read within a minute or so. A first read to check, not advice to rely on.

Is it cheaper? Mostly, no.

Can one PC really serve ten people?

Yes, for everyday use. Ten staff are rarely all asking at the same moment. Usually two to four are waiting on an answer at once. The free tool most people start with (Ollama) copes with about five at once. Past that you need a more capable setup, and it queues the rest.

The limit is memory. Every person working on a long document takes a slice of the card’s memory. On the smaller cards, several people summarising big files at once will slow everyone down.

The verdict

Possible, but it is not a money-saver at this size. The figures above assume a machine between A$5,000 and A$10,000, A$0.30 a unit for power and A$150 an hour for IT time. Change them and the answer moves, but not by much for ten people.

The strong reason to do this is privacy and control, not cost. Cost only starts to favour your own machine with many more users, or heavy daily use that would run into usage limits on the paid tools.

Is it viable for my company?

A simple way to decide.

Run through these. If most of the left column sounds like you, it is worth a trial.

Probably a good fit if

  • Client or patient information must not leave your premises
  • You are in law, health, finance or government work with strict rules
  • You have someone in-house or on contract who can look after it
  • Your staff mainly summarise, draft and look things up in documents
  • You want a fixed cost that does not climb with each new user

Probably not if

  • You already use Microsoft 365 and want AI inside Outlook and Teams
  • Your people need the most capable AI for hard analysis or coding
  • Nobody has time to keep it patched, backed up and running
  • You mainly want to save money on a team of ten or fewer
  • You need current news or live web answers

Try it before you buy anything

  1. Borrow a PC with a 12 GB or bigger graphics card, or use one you already own.
  2. Install the free tools (Ollama and Open WebUI) and load one of the models above.
  3. Give it to three people for two weeks on real work, then ask whether they would miss it.
